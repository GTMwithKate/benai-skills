# Copywriting: slide count, arc, budgets, voice

## Slide count (3-10)

Pick the count from the content, not from habit. Typical is 5-8; fewer lands harder.

- **3-4 slides**: one sharp claim with one proof. Hook, one substance slide, CTA.
- **5-6 slides**: a method with 2-3 steps, or a claim with two proofs.
- **7-8 slides**: a full framework, list, or before/after walkthrough.
- **9-10 slides**: only when the source genuinely has that many distinct, useful beats. Padding kills completion rate.

If you are cutting material to hit a count, you chose the right count. If you are stretching, go smaller.

## The arc (do not make every slide a dramatic header)

1. **Opening slides (1 to ~3) — big-header, high-impact.** Cover/hook, problem, first turn toward the solution. One large headline + one accent highlight + a short body line. The cover is what sells the swipe: it must promise a specific payoff, not a topic.
2. **Middle slides — text-heavier substance.** Deliver the actual method so the carousel stands alone: a smaller header plus 2-4 short bullets, numbered steps, or a simple labeled comparison (before/after, two columns). One idea per slide. This is what makes the carousel worth saving.
3. **Final slide — a CTA that makes sense.** Follow, watch the full breakdown, save this, or comment/DM a keyword for the resource. Never a CTA that leans on context the slides never gave.

**Self-contained rule:** assume the caption is one line. Whatever the point is, the slides must actually teach or prove it. If the source only teases, reconstruct the real substance into the middle slides.

## Slot budgets

| Slot | Budget |
|------|--------|
| Eyebrow | 2-5 words, uppercase, letter-spaced |
| Headline | 4-9 words (hook slides), up to ~12 (substance slides) |
| Highlight | 1-3 consecutive words inside the headline; the punchline, never a connector |
| Body (hook slides) | 1-2 fragment lines, ~10 words total |
| Bullets (substance slides) | 2-4 bullets, ~3-7 words each |
| CTA line | One imperative sentence |

Budgets exist because slides are read at phone size in under two seconds each. When copy fights the budget, cut the copy, not the type size.

## Voice

**If the design system defines a voice** (`design-system/foundations/voice.md` and `vocabulary.md`), those rules win. Read them before writing a word and follow the do/don't pairs.

**Default voice when the brand defines none:**

- Second person, direct, practitioner tone. State the claim, then back it up.
- Short punches. Specific verbs, real nouns, numbers when the source has them.
- No buzzwords (leverage, robust, seamless, comprehensive, delve), no hedges (might, perhaps, in some cases), no corporate abstractions (solutions, ecosystem, synergy).
- Prefer periods, commas, and colons over em dashes; restructure rather than splice.

## CTA framings to draw from

- Follow: "Follow for the next one."
- Watch: "Watch the full breakdown." / "The step-by-step is on YouTube."
- Save: "Save this before you need it."
- Get: "Comment '{keyword}' for the template." / "DM '{keyword}' for the guide."

Pick the one the content earns. If the carousel taught a method, "watch the full breakdown" or "grab the template" usually beats a generic follow.
