# Reference sources per use case

Where to send the user (and yourself) to find what good looks like. Show 3-5 relevant sources at Step 4, each with one line on what to pull from it. Every URL below was live as of 2026-08-28. Fold in every source a user supplies.

**The moodboard method** (works for every use case): collect 2-3 references, then deconstruct WHY each works — focal point, type, density, color energy — before generating anything. The recipe you land on is what goes into the user's skill. **Savee** (savee.com) and **Pinterest** are the fastest general-purpose moodboard builders: search a keyword, save a board, work from screenshots.

## Websites and landing pages

- **Dribbble** (dribbble.com) - polished visual direction; search "[industry] landing page". Treat as mood, not literal builds — most shots are concepts.
- **Framer gallery** (framer.com/gallery) - real, working marketing sites; strong for section rhythm and motion restraint.
- **Figma Community** (figma.com/community) - landing page and site templates with inspectable layers.
- **Awwwards** (awwwards.com) - the high end; good for ambition, filter hard for what a business site actually needs.
- **Land-book** (land-book.com) - large landing-page archive, filterable by industry and page type (pricing, about).
- **Recent.design** (recent.design) - curated design-engineer taste (the site formerly known as Godly; godly.website now redirects here).
- **One Page Love** (onepagelove.com) - curated single-page sites; ideal for landing pages specifically.
- **SaaS Landing Page** (saaslandingpage.com) - SaaS pages organized by section (hero, pricing, testimonials); best when copying one block.
- **Curated.design** - fine-grained filters: style, section, even font.
- **SiteInspire** (siteinspire.com) / **Lapa Ninja** (lapa.ninja) / **Dark.design** - deeper galleries: by style, by volume, and dark-UI-only.

## Components and app UI

- **21st.dev** - copyable React/Tailwind marketing and UI components; use for hero/pricing/feature-section building blocks.
- **shadcn/ui** (ui.shadcn.com) - the canonical open-source component registry; its blocks double as reference designs. **shadcnblocks.com** adds hundreds of prebuilt marketing blocks.
- **Mobbin** (mobbin.com) - real product UI screenshots from live apps, organized by pattern; the reference for flows and app screens.
- **Page Flows** (pageflows.com) - recorded real user flows (onboarding, checkout, upgrade) as video, not stills.
- **UI Garage** (uigarage.net) / **Nicely Done** (nicelydone.club) - pattern-by-pattern collections (empty states, tables, pricing).
- **Design Spells** (designspells.com) - delightful micro-interactions; use for polish ideas.
- **scrnshts** (scrnshts.club) - the reference for app store screenshot design.

## Email

- **milled.com** - searchable archive of real brand sends; search by brand or goal keyword ("launch", "welcome", "40% off"). Reality, not curation - sift.
- **reallygoodemails.com** - curated, categorized by email type (announcement, newsletter, promo, welcome); many entries include the actual HTML.
- **Email Love** (emaillove.com) - curated email and newsletter inspiration by category and trend.
- **Pinterest** - visual direction for email ("[industry] email design"); treat pins as palette/density/type attitude, not as client-safe layouts.

## Infographics

- **LinkedIn** - search the topic and sort by engagement; what practitioners actually save and repost.
- **Pinterest** - "infographic [topic]" for layout variety; strongest for dense teaching formats.
- **Reddit** - r/coolguides (explainer guides), r/dataisbeautiful (data viz, sort by top-of-month), r/infographics (general).
- **Instagram** - carousel-adjacent single graphics; good for quote cards and stat cards.
- **Information is Beautiful** (informationisbeautiful.net) - the benchmark for editorial infographics.
- **Dataviz Inspiration** (dataviz-inspiration.com) - curated dataviz projects browsable by chart type.
- **The Pudding** (pudding.cool) / **FlowingData** (flowingdata.com) - the high end of visual storytelling and chart craft.

## Carousels

- **LinkedIn** - the native habitat; collect carousels from creators in the user's niche and note slide counts and hook patterns. Buffer's annotated roundup (buffer.com/resources/linkedin-carousel-examples/) explains why specific ones work.
- **Instagram** - swipe posts from design-forward accounts; stronger on visual variety, weaker on B2B substance.
- **Pinterest** - "carousel design" templates for composition ideas.
- **Figma Community** - search "LinkedIn carousel" for swipe files with inspectable slide structures.

## Slide decks and presentations

- **Slidebean pitch deck gallery** (slidebean.com/pitch-deck-examples) - recreated decks of real funded startups; the most-cited pitch reference.
- **Figma Community** - search "pitch deck" or "presentation"; inspectable deck templates.
- **Pitch** (pitch.com) - polished template gallery plus design-principle write-ups.
- **Dribbble** - search "pitch deck" for slide-layout style direction.
- **SlideShare** (slideshare.net) - huge archive; mine it for content structure, not visual style.

## Dashboards

- **SaaS Interface** (saasinterface.com/pages/dashboard/) - 150+ real SaaS dashboard screenshots, categorized.
- **Mobbin** - real analytics screens from live products; the honest reference for what survives daily use.
- **Dribbble** - tags "dashboard", "analytics"; direction for density and card design (concepts, not proof).
- The user's own paid tools - Stripe, Linear, Vercel and similar set the modern standard most users mean by "clean".
- For the chart layer under the chrome: Dataviz Inspiration, FlowingData, Information is Beautiful (see Infographics).

## Icons

- **Lucide** (lucide.dev) - the default library: consistent 1.5px-stroke outline icons, permissively licensed.
- **Phosphor** (phosphoricons.com) - larger set, multiple weights, when Lucide lacks a glyph.
- **Heroicons** (heroicons.com) - solid/outline pairs from the Tailwind team.
- One library per skill. Mixed icon sets read as unfinished.

## Brand and app logos

- **svgl.app** - live API for real, current app logos as SVG (`https://api.svgl.app?search=<name>` returns routes; each route serves the vector). When an output names a tool (Gmail, Slack, Notion...), use the real mark from here - never redraw or approximate a third-party logo. Marks name the tool; they never imply endorsement.
- **Simple Icons** (simpleicons.org) - 3k+ monochrome brand SVGs with official brand colors listed.
- Fallbacks: gilbarbara/logos (GitHub), vectorlogo.zone. For a company with no published vector, use a bordered name chip at the same height as neighboring marks, and say so at handover.

## Fonts and colors

- **Google Fonts** (fonts.google.com) - webfont pairing and loading; the design system decides the faces, this is where they load from.
- **Typewolf** (typewolf.com) / **Fonts In Use** (fontsinuse.com) - real sites and real work organized by typeface; the practitioner favorites for type-in-context.
- **Fontpair** (fontpair.co) / **Fontjoy** (fontjoy.com) - curated and generated Google Fonts pairings.
- **Coolors** (coolors.co) - palette exploration and contrast checking when a design system is being extended (never to invent brand colors that belong to the Step 2 gate).
- **Realtime Colors** (realtimecolors.com) - previews a palette + font on a realistic mockup, with contrast checks.

## Always, at the end

Ask the user for their own sources: creators they follow, competitors they respect, past outputs they were proud of, boards they keep. A user-supplied reference outranks everything above, and it belongs in their generated skill's references file so every future run starts from it.
